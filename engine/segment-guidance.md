# Segment Guidance — Universal Engine

Answers "what sells well in [city]?" and "which segment should we target?" questions.
Always load alongside the city profile — city profile DTS and demand bonuses override
national baselines wherever they differ.

---

## How to answer a segment question

1. Load the city profile's segment velocity table (Section 2 in all profiles)
2. Cross-reference with the universal segment traits below
3. Output: top 3–5 segments for that market, ranked by GPD potential
4. For each segment: why it works in that city, what spec to target, what to avoid
5. Flag any segments the city profile marks as slow or "avoid"

## Universal segment index

### Jeep Wrangler / Gladiator
**National DTS baseline:** 14–18d
**Who buys:** Outdoor lifestyle, off-road enthusiasts, younger buyers
**What drives velocity:**
- Hard top vs. soft top: hard top sells 3–5d faster in cold/snow markets
- Color: White and Black fastest everywhere; green/orange/yellow add 4–6d
- Trim: Sahara/Rubio command real premium over Sport; Willys is regional
- Uconnect 5 (12.3") vs. base screen: meaningful buyer signal, $400 retail adj
- 4WD is standard — no drivetrain split
**Risk factors:** High theft target (Wrangler doors/tops); confirm VIN on CARFAX
**City profile overlay:** SLC adds $1,200 flat demand bonus; check local outdoor recreation culture

### HMA Factory OEM SUV (Tucson, Santa Fe, Santa Cruz, Palisade, Telluride)
**National DTS baseline:** 18–22d
**Who buys:** Value-conscious families, tech workers, first-time SUV buyers
**What drives velocity:**
- 10/100 warranty remaining: closes deals — apply 6% retail bonus
- 5/60 warranty: good but not exceptional — 3.5% retail bonus
- AWD spec: 3–5d faster than FWD in mountain/snow markets; neutral elsewhere
- Limited/XRT trim preferred; SE/SEL mid-tier sell but slower
- Hampton Gray, White, Black all fast; Rockwood Green adds 4d in most markets
**Risk factors:** Inspection-to-auction gap often 40–55d (HMA OEM pipeline) — flag battery and TPMS on arrival
**City profile overlay:** Always check city-specific AWD premium; warranty bonus is consistent across all profiles

### Toyota / Honda AWD Crossover (CR-V, RAV4, Highlander, Venza, Pilot)
**National DTS baseline:** 18–24d
**Who buys:** Reliability-first buyers, families, high-trust brand loyalists
**What drives velocity:**
- AWD non-negotiable in mountain/snow markets — FWD copies can sit 10–14d longer
- Mileage ceiling: < 30k miles moves fast; 30–50k still strong; > 60k slows
- Hybrid variants command $1,500–$2,500 retail premium and sell faster in eco markets (Seattle, Portland)
- Color: White and Silver fastest; no meaningful penalty colors except brown/champagne
**Risk factors:** MMR is firm — Toyota/Honda buyers know market value; don't expect to bid far below floor
**City profile overlay:** 15% markup base rate; SLC treats RAV4 AWD as gold standard

---

### Full-Size 4WD Truck (F-150, Silverado, Sierra, Ram 1500 — SLE+/XLT+ trim)
**National DTS baseline:** 20–26d
**Who buys:** Work buyers, outdoor/recreation buyers, lifestyle buyers
**What drives velocity:**
- 4WD essential in mountain and northern markets; RWD trucks sit 8–12d longer
- Trim matters: SLE/XLT and above move fastest; base Work Truck is fleet buyer only
- Crew cab outsells extended cab 3:1 in most retail markets
- White and Black fastest; specialty colors (Burnt Orange, etc.) add 5–8d
- Mileage ceiling: < 50k retail; 50–80k still moves but requires price positioning
- 2026 tariff tailwind: domestic truck is a strong buy signal — buyers paying a premium for domestic vs. import uncertainty
**Risk factors:** High gross opportunity but also high floor risk — sellers know what trucks are worth
**City profile overlay:** Always check domestic + AWD tariff bonus; SLC buyers actually use trucks for work/recreation

---

### Full-Size 3-Row SUV (Grand Cherokee L, Palisade, Telluride, Traverse, Durango, Expedition)
**National DTS baseline:** 24–30d
**Who buys:** Large families, road-trip buyers, towing buyers
**What drives velocity:**
- 3-row is the spec — demand collapses without it (confirm seating from PDF page 4)
- AWD/4WD strongly preferred in mountain markets; FWD is harder to move
- Trim: Limited/SEL and above; base trim has limited buyer pool
- 2026 tariff tailwind on domestic units (Jeep, Chevy, Ford)
**Risk factors:** Longer DTS than crossovers — flooring cost matters in dealer mode; less urgent in client mode
**City profile overlay:** SLC adds +8% MMR demand bonus for 3-row (outsized LDS family demand); check each city profile for local family size data

### Minivan (Pacifica, Carnival, Sienna, Odyssey)
**National DTS baseline:** 28–35d
**Who buys:** Large families, young parents, LDS demographic (SLC specifically)
**What drives velocity:**
- Pacifica Touring L is the velocity trim; base Touring is slower
- AWD not available on most minivans — not a disqualifier in this segment
- Low mileage premium: < 20k miles commands real price separation
- Carnival competes directly with Pacifica at lower price point — know local preference
- Sienna hybrid rare at auction but fastest-selling when found
**Risk factors:** Buyer pool is narrower than crossovers; get pricing right or it sits
**City profile overlay:** SLC adds +8% MMR demand bonus (largest family size metro in US); check other cities for LDS community presence before assuming elevated demand

### Compact AWD Sport (Subaru Outback, Crosstrek, Forester — AWD standard)
**National DTS baseline:** 20–26d
**Who buys:** Outdoor lifestyle, reliability buyers, younger demographics
**What drives velocity:**
- AWD is standard on Subaru — no drivetrain filter needed
- LDS Church Fleet units (Element/CPB) add institutional trust signal — fastest-moving Outbacks
- Limited and Touring trims preferred; base trim has narrower buyer pool
- Color: White, Silver fastest; avoid brown/dark green
**Risk factors:** Subaru buyers are informed — they know market value; no padding above MMR without strong justification
**City profile overlay:** 18% markup base; SLC Outback = prime retail profile

### Sports Car / Enthusiast (GR86, BRZ, GR Supra, Miata, Mustang GT, Civic Type R)
**National DTS baseline:** 24–35d (segment-dependent)
**Who buys:** Enthusiast buyers, weekend drivers, younger buyers
**What drives velocity by model:**
- GR86 / BRZ: 20–24d nationally — supply-constrained, cult following, no color penalty
- GR Supra 3.0: 28–32d — narrow demographic but real price ceiling
- Mustang GT/EcoBoost Premium (fastback): 22–28d — broad appeal, RWD is fine here
- Civic Type R: 22–26d — hatch form, enthusiast + practical overlap
- Miata RF: 28–32d / Soft top: 34–40d — seasonal in cold/snow markets
- Dodge Challenger/Charger V8: 45–55d — discontinued, inventory-heavy, RWD penalty in snow markets (avoid)
**Risk factors:** Enthusiast buyers inspect more carefully; condition items matter more; mileage sensitivity higher
**City profile overlay:** GR86/BRZ fast in SLC (supply constrained); Miata adds winter DTS penalty in SLC; check each city for sports car buyer depth

### AWD Hot Hatch / Performance Compact (Golf R, Focus RS if found)
**National DTS baseline:** 24–30d
**Who buys:** Driving enthusiasts who want practicality
**What drives velocity:**
- AWD is the key spec — Golf GTI (FWD) is slower in snow markets
- Supply is low at auction — when found, often draws focused bidding
- Mileage: < 20k miles commands strong premium; > 40k narrows buyer pool fast
**Risk factors:** European brand financing friction (−8 confidence universal); buyer pool is narrower nationally
**City profile overlay:** SLC Golf R at 26d — AWD + winter-capable sports car = real demand; check city financing friction before bidding aggressively

### European Luxury (BMW, Mercedes, Audi, Volvo, Porsche, Land Rover)
**National DTS baseline:** 40–55d
**Who buys:** Affluent buyers; credit union lenders more selective
**What drives velocity:**
- BMW M-series and AMG command national interest but still slow in smaller markets
- Porsche 911 / Cayenne: fastest in luxury class when found under market
- Volvo XC90 / XC60 AWD: family-practical, moves faster than German equivalents
- Land Rover: reliability reputation is a headwind regardless of condition
**Risk factors:** European brand −8 confidence universal; financing friction at most credit unions; deferred maintenance is expensive and common
**City profile overlay:** Adjust for local luxury market depth; SLC east bench has real money but pool is thin — target only if MMR gap is wide

---

### Sport Sedan (Sonata N-Line, K5 GT, Accord Sport, Camry XSE)
**National DTS baseline:** 30–40d
**Who buys:** Value buyers who want style; younger buyers
**What drives velocity:**
- N-Line / GT / Sport trims only — base sedans are slow everywhere
- Red exterior is an asset on sport sedans (unlike SUVs)
- Manual transmission: small premium with right buyer, larger risk everywhere else
**Risk factors:** Sedan segment declining nationally; unless it's a distinct performance trim, buyer pool is shallow
**City profile overlay:** Check local sedan demand; SLC skews SUV/truck — sport sedans are tertiary buyers here

---

### Electric Vehicle (Ioniq 5/6, Kia EV6, Tesla Model 3/Y, Bolt)
**National DTS baseline:** 25–40d (highly market-dependent)
**Who buys:** Tech-forward buyers, commuters, dual-car households
**What drives velocity:**
- Tesla Model Y is fastest in class nationally — brand loyalty + charging network
- Ioniq 5/6 and EV6: fast in markets with HMA brand trust (SLC, Seattle, Denver)
- Bolt: value buyer — moves in price-sensitive markets
- Charging infrastructure matters: EV demand drops in rural or sprawl markets without Supercharger coverage
**Risk factors:** EV MMR has compressed significantly since 2023; depreciation risk is real; battery warranty status matters
**City profile overlay:** Check local EV infrastructure before targeting; Seattle/Portland EV demand is well above national average; SLC is moderate (Utah has state EV incentives)

| Segment | Reason |
|---|---|

| Land Rover (any) | Reliability reputation destroys retail trust |

## Output format for segment questions

When asked "what sells well in [city]?", output:

1. **Header:** City + profile confidence tier
2. **Top 5 segments:** ranked by GPD potential, with city-specific DTS and demand notes
3. **Target spec for each:** what trim, drivetrain, mileage, and color to look for at auction
4. **What to avoid:** city-specific slow movers
5. **Current market note:** any active tariff, seasonal, or inventory conditions from city profile
